What's included

Everything you need, nothing you don't.

Deck and fence cleaning includes inspection, cleaning, and a recommendation on next steps. Stain and seal work itself is not included unless quoted separately.

Pre-job inspection

Before we touch the wood, we look for rot, loose boards, and protruding nails. Anything we spot, we flag — better to know before we apply pressure.

Wood-safe cleaning solution

Cleaner gets applied and given time to do its work. Wood does not respond well to brute force, so this step matters more than the rinse step.

Right pressure, right tip

Cedar, pine, and pressure-treated wood each get a different tip. Composite gets a different approach again. Pressure stays in a range that cleans without etching the surface.

Cleanup around the deck

Beds and grass around the deck or fence get rinsed of any cleaner runoff and debris.

Stain-readiness call

When we finish, we tell you whether the wood is ready for stain in 24, 48, or 72 hours, based on weather and what we just did to it.

Walk-around

Final walk before we pack up. Anything that needs another pass gets it.

Signs it's time

You probably need deck or fence cleaning if…

  • 1

    Wood looks gray or weathered instead of its original color.

  • 2

    Black or green spotting on deck boards, rails, or fence pickets.

  • 3

    The deck feels slick underfoot — that is algae, not just dust.

  • 4

    You are planning to re-stain or seal and the surface needs to be cleaned first.

FAQ

Deck & Fence Cleaning questions.

Can I stain it right after?

Wood usually needs 24 to 72 hours of dry time before staining or sealing, depending on humidity and how much sun it gets. We tell you what to expect for your deck.

Will pressure washing damage softer wood like cedar?

It can if the wrong tip and pressure are used. We adjust both for the wood — cedar and pine get treated more carefully than pressure-treated lumber.
